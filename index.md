I'm a researcher exploring how language, cognition, and learning intersect. I recently earned my PhD in Chinese Linguistics from Indiana University Bloomington. 

My research examines how people comprehend, process, and learn language, with particular interests in sentence & word processing and language learning. To investigate these questions, I use methods from behavioral and experimental psycholinguistics (reaction times and eye tracking), electrophysiology (ERPs), and computational modeling. I work with <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/chienjerlin/home" target="_blank">Dr. Chien-Jer Charles Lin</a> and <a href="https://www.nozarilab.com/" target="_blank">Dr. Bonnie Nozari</a> at Indiana University, as well as <a href="https://brain.bnu.edu.cn/English/Faculty/CurrentFaculty/Gzz/d7ea1219c8074270bba5c736af040ba7.htm" target="_blank">Dr. Taomei Guo</a> at Beijing Normal University. 

My work centers on three main areas:
1. Sentence processing and comprehension -- examining how people integrate different sources of linguistic information, such as syntax and prosody, to interpret language in real time; <br>
2. Word recognition and lexical processing -- investigating how Chinese children process, represent, and learn written words; <br>
3. Language and computational models -- using advances in Large Language Models and other computational approaches to investigate and model aspects of human language processing.
